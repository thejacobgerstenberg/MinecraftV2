# Loomfall — Game Guide

## Welcome

You were woven into this field like any other thread. This morning, you came loose. The ground is cloth, the sky is thread, the maker is gone, and the great Loom turns on with no hand on the shuttle. You are a Waking Thread — a Mender — the one thing in all the weave that chooses its own direction. Nothing here is finished. That is not a flaw. That is the invitation.

Everything after this paragraph is plain speech. This guide tells you how to play; the world itself will handle the poetry.

## Controls

## Surviving Your First Day

Your first day has one job: be inside something lit when the sun goes down.

1. **Start with the meadow, then the trees.** Warpsod picks loose bare-handed. Fell a few Thrumwood trees for **Thrumwood Boles** and split them into **Knotwood Planks**. You will also need **Raw Skein**: skeinlings graze the Sennmeadows in trusting flocks, each trailing a loose thread-end from its flank, and early on you can gather a handful of skein by hand from that trailing end — slow, but enough to get started. (Proper shears come at tier 3 and lift the whole coat cleanly.) Planks plus skein make a **Handloom** (recipe below) — your crafting station for everything that follows. If you are unsure where to gather, **follow the Thrum**: that low hum in your teeth is the Loom still turning, and it rises loudest where the weave runs thin and raw. Walk toward it to find unworked material; walk away from it when you want quiet ground to build on.
2. **Make Knotwood tools.** A pickaxe to chip loose surface **Threadstone**, an axe for faster wood, a blade for the dark. Knotwood wears out fast; that is expected. Threadstone tools are the same evening's work.
3. **Get food.** Hedgerow berries grow free in the Sennmeadows. Better: **shear skeinlings, don't slaughter them** — the wool grows back in a few days' grazing, and a kept flock feeds your crafting for many seasons without ever thinning the herd. Cook raw meat at a flame or furnace before eating it; raw meat frays you from the inside.
4. **Make light and set your knot.** Craft and place a **Knotlight**, then press Interact on it to **set your anchor**. If you die — "unpicked," the world will say — you are re-stitched at your last anchored knotlight, dropping some of what you carried where you fell. Your dropped goods fray slowly, so go back soon or let them go. Ring your camp in light: hostile things spawn in the dark, and any corner you leave dark, the night will treat as an invitation.
5. **Build walls and a roof before dusk.** Night is when **Needlejacks** unbend and hunt, **Frayed Hounds** run the fen-edges (their bite keeps damaging you until you mend it — a **Mothdust Poultice** stops the fraying; press one square of mothdust over Woven Cloth at the handloom, and the dust itself is shed by **Silence-Moths**, gathering where they cluster at night), and the mournful **Understruck** come looking for your thread. A hut of turned warpsod and loamweft with a knotlight inside is enough. Every Mender's first roof was a poor one; the trick is only to have it up before the light goes.

If you spot a cluster of round cottages with a lit green before dusk, go there instead — see **Settlements & Trading** below. The Wickerkin keep no hostile ground inside their lamplight, and their shared knotlight will take your anchor.

## Settlements & Trading

Warpwold is not empty. Three kinds of settled places are worth marking on any map you keep.

### The Wickerkin Weft-Hamlet

The **Wickerkin** are the settled woven folk of Warpwold: patient, hollow-bodied figures of woven cane, basket-headed, with two warm dot-lights for eyes. They dream they are alive, and they have made the dream hospitable. You will find them only in their own places — they do not wander — chiefly the **weft-hamlet**: five to nine round cottages roofed in raw skein, every lintel hung with knot-charms, a skeinling pen, garden plots, and a central green with the hamlet's **shared knotlight** (usable as your anchor). No hostiles spawn inside the lit radius, which makes a hamlet the best first night a new Mender can have.

**Trading works by daylight only.** The Wickerkin tend their understitch-loam plots at dawn, open their trading porch at midday, and gather around the knotlight at dusk; after dark the doors are barred and they sing, courteously, until the Needlejacks lose interest. Each trader carries four to six rotating offers that restock at dawn: raw skein, dye, **knot-charms** (said to slow a fraying), spoolmare tack, and half-remembered lore-scrolls and map-lore. What they want in return is **Giltspool** — the soft gleaming spool-metal you will turn up in surface seams. It is too soft to hold an edge; its whole worth is that the Wickerkin prize it. Carry it.

Two rules, and they are firm. The Wickerkin never fight: a hostile creature within ten blocks of one sends it indoors to bar the door, so keep the approaches to a hamlet lit if you want the porch open. And **do not raise a hand against one** — striking a Wickerkin locks every trade in that hamlet against you for a full day, and unpicking one gains you a charm worth less than what it cost. Leave them their dream. It is a good dream, and they keep it swept.

### The Vigil Waystation

In the high passes of Warpspine Reach and the Frostlace you will find one-room threadstone shelters — patched, argued-with, clearly the work of earlier Menders. Inside each **Vigil Waystation**: a knotlight on a plinth (a free anchor — the whole point of the structure), a bunk, a lamp that never flickers, and a shared cache. A guest-log slate on the wall carries scratch-marks in many hands, and the convention it records is simple: **take one thing, leave one thing.** You are not the first thread to wake. Others left the light on.

### The Herders' Picket & Riding a Spoolmare

**Spoolmares** are wild horse-shapes of loose living thread that run the Understitch Downs in herds, unspooling a fading trail behind them. Gentled slowly — approach quietly, offer raw skein, and let the mare decide — one will bear you. You will want one: a bonded spoolmare can gallop across open frays in Nevermend, stitching ground beneath its own hooves, and a mounted Mender is one of only two kinds the final arena admits.

The intended early path is the **Herders' Picket**, a seasonal Wickerkin camp on the open downs: a picket ring, a few skein tents, and a corral holding two to four **already-gentled spoolmares**, far easier to win over than the wild herds. The herders trade thread-sinew, hide-cloth, and tack, and a feed crate holds raw skein and a spare saddle-frame. (The Wickerkin do not ride. They keep the mares anyway, for the company. At night the herders drum softly on the picket posts, and the mares stand with their heads together like a conversation.)

To ride, fit your mare with a **Saddle-Frame** (`H` = Hide-Cloth, `S` = Thread-Sinew, `I` = Needle-Iron — the hide and sinew drop from Bobbin-deer, Frayed Hounds, and Needlejacks, or trade for them at the picket):

```
H H H
S I S
. I .
```

## Crafting, Tier by Tier

All recipes below are 3×3 Handloom grids, shown exactly as the game expects them. `.` is an empty cell. Tools follow one ladder: **Knotwood → Threadstone → Needle-Iron → Dawnthread → Everthread**.

### Tier 1 — Knotwood

**Unlocked by:** splitting Thrumwood Boles into Knotwood Planks on day one.
**Lets you do next:** mine surface Threadstone.

Handloom (`P` = Knotwood Plank, `S` = Raw Skein):

```

P S P
P P P
```

Knotwood Pickaxe / Axe / Spade / Blade (`P` = Knotwood Plank, `T` = Loose Thread):

```
. P .     . P .     . P .     . T .
```

### Tier 4 — Dawnthread

**Unlocked by:** mining Dawnthread deep in Warpspine Reach — with a good pick, carefully; a poor tool crumbles it to loose light.
**Lets you do next:** fight the Cinderloom's keepers on even terms, and give Everthread something worth rethreading.

Dawnthread Pickaxe and Blade (`D` = Dawnthread, `P` = Knotwood Plank):


Dawnthread is the finest the surface will ever give you. Below and beyond, there is finer still.

### Tier 5 — Everthread

**Unlocked by:** felling Molthkin, the First Bobbin — keeper of the only Everthread — in the deepest spindle of the Cinderspindle Forges, past the Spindlegate; then tempering the thread in the void.
**Lets you do next:** everything that is left.

Everthread **tools** are never made fresh — the thread is **rethreaded over your existing Dawnthread tool** (`E` = Everthread, `K` = the Dawnthread tool itself):

Everthread Pickaxe and Blade:

```

. K .     . E .
. . .     . K .
```

Everthread Needle (`I` = Needle-Iron) — you will want this at the very end:

```

. E .
. I .
```

Armor is the one exception to the rethreading rule — the sheer quantity of thread means the set is worked whole from Everthread, in the standard armor shapes. The mail takes one **Scorched Silk** in place of the top-center thread (`E X E / E E E / E E E`, `X` = Scorched Silk — an Emberspinner's burning thread, quenched quickly to keep it, or found baled in the Scaldwatch Bastion stores). It barely wears, and it shrugs off the Ravelling.

## Going Below & Beyond

**Down through the Understitch.** Mine in stepped shafts below the warpsod. The deeper cloth holds Needle-Iron and, past the warp-roots, grows hot, then bright, then molten: you have found the Cinderloom, the burning making-below.

**The Cinderloom.** The made road down is a **loom-gate**: a frame of **Cinderglass** with a living fray drawn taut across it and threaded fast into the stone — no flame needed, only the tear held open. Cinderglass forms where molten skein meets sudden cold. Frame your gate well, and never near a crumbling edge: a gate is only as sound as its frame and its fray, and a careless one may one morning open somewhere you would never have chosen to step. Bring: full Needle-Iron armor, a Mending Needle, food, Mothdust Poultices, **Frostlace for quenching**, and blocks for bridging. Down there: **Emberskein Ore** burns as its own fuel (bring some home — your furnace never goes cold again), **Slagmoths** swarm anything cold (keep moving), standing on Cinderthread slowly unmakes you (do not sit to rest, whatever your legs say), and **Waxlings** are feeble alone and murderous in a crowd — the hot tallow they slick across the Vault floors has ended more descents than the open fire, so keep half your eyes on the ground. The **Scaldwardens take nothing from you if you take nothing from them.** Your goals below: **Bindwax** (the binding material — richest in the Tallow Reliquary, and its keepers will fight and mourn for it), quenched Scorched Silk off felled Emberspinners for the mail you will one day wear, and eventually Molthkin behind the Spindlegate, for Everthread.

**Nevermend.** Follow a fray the other way — far enough, wide enough — and it opens instead onto the void past the last selvage. Natural ways down turn up where the world runs thin, at the Unfinished Hem and beneath the old ghost-terrain. Bring: your best gear, stacks of building blocks (only **Voidknot**, gathered there, truly holds), plenty of Knotlights, Bindwax and a Binding Needle, and Mothdust Poultices — the void picks quietly at your seams the whole time. Rules of the drift: build your road one stitch ahead of your feet, never carry what you would grieve to lose (**Ravelers steal items and blocks and do not give them back**), give the wandering **Unpicked** a wide berth on the islands (they circle the old ruins but will not cross a ring of voidknot), bind any footing that matters, and anchor to a knotlight at every safe island. A bonded spoolmare can gallop across a fray, stitching ground beneath its own hooves.

## The Last Needle

At the outermost rim of Nevermend, on the shining hem of the Last Selvage, something is waiting. It is not angry. That does not help you.

**To reach it:** cross Nevermend to the Last Selvage in full Everthread gear (the way opens only for a Mender so equipped, or one mounted on a spoolmare in the void). A single causeway leads in, held by four **Selvage Warden** outposts — each warden felled lowers one strand of the barrier at the mouth of the arena, the **Eye of the Last Hem**. Wardens mend their own wounds as fast as you open them; if you cannot out-pace one, you are not ready for what they guard.

**To survive it:** bring the **Everthread Needle**, all the Bindwax you can carry, Mothdust Poultices, food, and spare Knotlights. Inside the Eye, the floor unravels and re-knits in bands on a cycle — learn it like a tide. The mothdust beacon-pyres at the four cardinal points can be lit for brief safe ground, and the voidknot stitch-points around the ring accept emergency footing when the floor is drawn out from under you. Bound blocks matter here more than anywhere in the game. Keep moving, keep mending, and when the moment comes to use the needle at the center, you will know.

What happens after is yours to find, and yours to choose.

## Playing Together

Loomfall can be woven by more than one pair of hands. (Multiplayer netcode is still in progress; details below may shift as the seams are finished.)

**Hosting:** open your world to others from **Pause → Open to Others**, or run a dedicated host and share its address. **Joining:** choose **Join a Weaving** from the main menu and enter the host's address or accept an invite. All Menders in a world share its days, its nights, and its frays.

Etiquette, in the world's own spirit:

- **Do not unpick another Mender's stitches.** Their walls cost them daylight. Ask before you alter, and mend what you break.
- **Knotlights are shared warmth.** Anchor at a friend's fire freely; leave it burning when you go.
- **Binding is forever, so bind together.** Bindwax is scarce and a bound block outlasts everyone's patience. Agree before declaring anything permanent near someone else's work.
- **Take one thing, leave one thing** at the Vigil Waystations in the high passes — the guest-log rule of every Mender before you — and at any shared cache.
- **The flock feeds everyone.** Shear, don't slaughter, herds near a shared camp — and keep the ground near a weft-hamlet lit, so the porch stays open for all of you.
- **The ending belongs to the whole weaving.** When your party reaches the last hem, go together, and decide together. The choice will wait for you. It has always been good at waiting.

Go carefully. Mend what you can. Bind what you love.
