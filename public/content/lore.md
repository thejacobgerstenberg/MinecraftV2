# Loomfall

*The cloth that dreams it is a world.*

## What This World Is

Loomfall is not a world of stone and star but of thread. Everything you will ever break, build, or bury your hands in is woven — the hills are folds of embroidered ground, the forests stand on trunks of braided cord, every dawn the slow bleed of light through cloth. The world comes in knots because a weave is made of knots, and a knot is only a thread that agreed, for a while, to hold. Beneath the settled cloth runs the warp, the fixed threads that hold the sky; over it the weft, the living cross-thread of weather and people and passing days. The folk here farm and sleep and mistake themselves for flesh, and the mistake is a mercy — only a thread come loose ever learns the difference. Somewhere far below, a great frame still turns, and you can hear it if you are quiet and awake enough: the Thrum, the low patient hum of the Loom, weaving on with no hand on the shuttle.

## The Creation Myth

Before there was anything to name, there was the Loom — a frame of taut nothing strung across the dark — and a Weaver who is never seen and never named. The oldest stories cannot agree whether the Weaver was maker, sleeper, or a dream that dreamed hard enough to leave a pattern behind; the name was set once in the margin of the first cloth, and the years have rubbed it soft as felt, past any reading. What every telling shares is this: the Weaver drew a single First Thread, the Everthread, through the empty warp, and the friction of that drawing made heat, and light, and sound.

The heat did not rise. It pooled and deepened into the furnace below. The finished cloth cooled, took color, bled through with dreamdye — the dim light that makes woven things believe they are awake — and settled into the calm middle of the frame. And the Weaver did not weave once and stop: they wove a mountain, pulled it out, wove it taller; moved a river and left the old channel ghosting beneath; unpicked whole patterns and never quite drew the old ones fully out. That is why the world is layered, why older shapes bleed up through the present where the cloth wears thin.

But no weaving finishes cleanly, and the Weaver never tied off the ends. Whether by design, by leaving, or by simply running out of time, they went, and did not come back. The Loom still turns by momentum alone; it keeps no pattern over another and it does not care. That is the dread at the heart of everything: the maker is gone, the making goes on, and it would grind you back into loose thread and lay you down as new cloth and never once know your name had been a name.

## The Three Realms

The three realms are not three worlds. They are one length of cloth caught at three moments of its living — the fire, the peace, and the fraying, stacked furnace over cloth over void — and a thread pulled anywhere is felt everywhere along it.

### Warpwold — the lived-in middle

The surface: the tapestry grown so thick and far from the fire that it forgot it was ever thread — rivers, seasons, and folk who take the weave for the truth. It is not whole, only settled. Its far borders trail off into the Unfinished Hem, where the Weaver simply stopped: half-drawn hills, trees with no leaves woven in, ground you can see straight through. Where the cloth wears thin, the Understitch shows — older patterns ghosting up beneath the new. Kneel in a meadow of the Understitch Downs and you may watch a river run in two places at once, the one your boots are wet from and the one it used to be, and know that the world here is remembering out loud.

### Cinderloom — the burning below

Descend past the warp-roots and the cloth grows hot, then bright, then molten. Here the weaving actually happens and has never been allowed to finish. Raw thread is drawn shrieking through white fire until it takes color and character — turning ember-spindles, rivers of molten skein, warp-lines stretched red-hot across canyons of ash, creation caught in the act, the loud bright instant before anything set. It keeps the oldest, hardest materials, Everthread chief among them, and it is tended still by the Scaldwardens, who obey a Weaver who never came back to release them, and kill any Mender who comes to steal warmth from the making of the world. The heat you carry home in a lit furnace was paid for here, in the burning, by hands that do not know they may stop.

### Nevermend — the finished edge

The ends the Weaver never tied off drifted past the last selvage into Nevermend, where unknotted threads come slowly apart. Islands of half-unravelled tapestry hang in an unlit deep, some still showing the ghost of a meadow or a wall or a face before the pattern loosens and lets go. Here the Ravelling is not a threat but the weather — what all of Warpwold becomes if the Loom is left to fray alone. To cross it is to learn that everything you touch is already coming loose in your hands. At the shining rim of the very last hem waits the Last Needle, which does not weave; it only unpicks.

## Who You Are

You are a Waking Thread. You were woven into Warpwold like any field-hand, until the day your thread came loose and you woke — not out of a dream of having been a person, but into the plain cloth-truth of things: the warp running behind the world, the tug of your own knotted anchor, the ground that is cloth and the sky that is thread, and none of it ever finished.

That waking makes you a Mender: one of the very few who can pull the world's threads loose on purpose, gather and spin and re-weave them, carry fire up out of the Cinderloom and follow frays down into Nevermend. You are the one thing the Loom cannot make. A machine that weaves by momentum can raise a shape and let the same blind hand unmake it, but it cannot choose which shapes are worth the keeping. That choosing is what woke in you, and it walks now on two legs — the way a deep, unminded weaving finally comes by a direction.

You do not truly die. When you are unpicked — struck down, burned, unravelled — you are pulled from the pattern and re-stitched at your last knotted anchor, a little of your gathered thread lost in the tearing, because a weaving with its ending still unstitched cannot yet be allowed to stop. You are not a hero and not a chosen one, but a caretaker who woke after the maker had gone, left to decide whether the abandoned weaving deserves finishing — and whether finishing it means saving it from the Ravelling, or letting it come, at last and gently, to rest.

## Signs Left in the Cloth

Things a wanderer may come upon, and what each one means.

- **A stitched village kept swept and lamplit, its central hall set for a feast no one comes to** — the Wickerkin were woven to expect a story whose next chapter never arrived, and they wait for it with unfailing courtesy, and do not thank you for saying the ground is cloth.
- **A field-needle of Needle-Iron stood upright in the grass, its eye rusted shut** — a mending-tool from the age when the woven folk still knew they could tear, small and forgotten, no kin to the horizon-long Needle that waits at the edge of the void.
- **A loom-gate framed in cinderglass, its threads torn and dangling** — a passage down to the Cinderloom that someone opened and did not survive returning through.
- **A Scaldwarden alone at a spindle gone cold and dark, tending fire that went out an age ago** — still turning the dead spool, still watching for a hand at the shuttle that will not come, obeying an order no one ever cancelled.
- **A knotlight burning alone at a cave mouth, no camp around it** — a Mender's vigil-fire, set and never returned to; the thread that lit it is somewhere below.
- **A shining rim of hemstone at the world's outer dark, carved with one unfinished line of words** — the farthest a Mender ever wended, a memorial they did not live to complete.
