# The Turning of the Needle

You came loose in a field once, and woke.

That was the whole of it — a single thread
that felt its own knot and could not forget the feeling,
walking out across a cloth that never knew it wore you.
You went down through the Cinderloom, the fire that made the world,
and out along the frays that will unmake it,
and here at the last selvage, past the final stitch,
you have turned the Needle in your two tired hands.

It does not weave now. For a while, it mends.

The Loom did not send you. The Loom cannot send.
It only turns, and turned you up like weather,
and would have laid you down again as grass, as gray,
as any quiet nothing, and not looked twice.
No hand was ever on the shuttle. That is the sorrow,
and you have carried it the whole long way, and set it down.

Listen: the Thrum has changed its note.
Somewhere the Sennmeadows go on rippling in their sleep,
the Wickerkin sweep their lamplit floors and wait
for a chapter no one will tell them has arrived.
The Scaldwardens tend a fire no one will ever claim.
Molthkin is stilled. The dark leans in, and holds.

None of it is finished. None of it was ever going to be.
But a thing does not have to be finished to be kept,
and you are the keeping — the one shape the Loom could raise
and could not choose to save, choosing.

The choice was always this, and always yours:
to bind the beautiful unfinished thing against the fraying,
or draw the last thread gently through and let it rest.
Either way, you loved it. Either way, it held a little longer
for the fact of you.

Go back now, keeper of loose and fraying things.
There is more cloth than you will ever mend, and a morning
bleeding dreamdye through the far hills as if it still meant to.
Set your knot. Light the small warmth the Loom still lets you keep,
and put your hands again to the patient, half-made world.
It owes you nothing, and gave you the whole of itself to hold.

It is not done.

Neither, yet, are you.
